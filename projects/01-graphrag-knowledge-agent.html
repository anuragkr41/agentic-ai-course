Enterprise Knowledge Intelligence Agent (GraphRAG)

A defensible system design for "ask anything across your enterprise knowledge" — combining vectors, graph, and rigorous explainability.

Prerequisites: M3 · M5 · M6 · M7 · M9 · M10 · Build time: 6–10 engineering weeks

Problem statement

A B2B SaaS company has accumulated 50,000+ documents — contracts, support tickets, policy docs, product specs, sales decks — spread across SharePoint, Confluence, Notion, and S3. Customer success teams, sales engineers, compliance officers, and external customers all ask cross-cutting questions: "Which clients are affected by clause changes in the new template?" "What\'s our SLA for refunds for enterprise tier customers in EMEA?" "Show me all conversations where customer X mentioned competitor Y." Naive vector RAG can find similar chunks but cannot connect entities across documents, cite sources reliably, or pass compliance review.

Why a founder builds this

  • Sales cycle compression. Sales engineers spend 30% of their time hunting for answers customers already asked. Closing that gap is worth 1–2 quota point per rep.
  • Enterprise deal enablement. Regulated buyers require explainable, auditable AI. Without source-cited answers, you lose RFPs.
  • Customer self-service. Reduce support tickets by 30–40% with grounded, cite-everything answers.
  • Compliance posture. When asked "how did the AI decide X," produce a reconstructable trace.

Architecture

Component breakdown

Design decisions you can defend

  • GraphRAG, not vector-only. Cross-document queries (clause changes, customer relationships, recall scope) require entity-level joining. Path explainability is a deal-closer for regulated buyers.
  • Ontology starts at 10 entity types. Customer, Account, Contract, Clause, Product, Order, SupportTicket, Conversation, Employee, Document. Grow only when queries demand more.
  • Three-lane hybrid retrieval. Vector for fuzzy meaning; BM25 for exact terms (product names, contract clause numbers); graph for relationships. Rerank merges.
  • Evidence-first answers. Schema requires citations. If the model can\'t cite, it returns "I don\'t have that information." Hallucination drops 60–80% by this rule alone.
  • Path field, not just citations. For multi-hop questions, the answer shows the entity traversal. Customer can verify; auditor can audit; debugger can spot extraction errors.
  • Default reranking. One extra API call per query (~$0.001), +15-20 point recall lift. Pays back immediately.
  • HITL is sparse. Don\'t gate every answer — kills throughput and trains reviewers to rubber-stamp. Gate when confidence < 0.7, when topic is regulated (legal, financial), or when the answer crosses 3+ entity types (high blast radius).

Interview / investor / customer talking points

Q: How is this different from "just RAG"?
A: Three differences. (1) Graph traversal for multi-hop queries — "which customers are affected by X" can\'t be answered by similarity. (2) Path-based explainability — every answer shows the entity chain that connects evidence, which compliance buyers require. (3) Triple-lane retrieval (vector + BM25 + graph) reranked together — significantly better recall on real queries.
Q: What\'s your eval discipline?
A: Four signals tracked weekly on a labeled set of 300 queries: retrieval recall@5, groundedness (LLM-as-judge calibrated against humans on 50 cases at >85% agreement), citation coverage (% of claims with sources), faithfulness (no contradictions to evidence). No prompt or model change ships without a regression check against this set.
Q: How do you prevent hallucination?
A: Three layers. Schema requires every claim to cite a chunk; no citation = refusal. Post-generation groundedness check via LLM-as-judge retries if anything is unsupported. Continuous monitoring of citation coverage and faithfulness — drift triggers review. We measure rather than hope.
Q: Why Neo4j over a property graph in Postgres?
A: Multi-hop traversal performance. Postgres can do graph queries with recursive CTEs, but at our document scale it\'s 10-100× slower than Neo4j on 3+ hop queries. We may switch back if Postgres\' graph features mature; for now Neo4j earns its keep on the structural queries.
Q: What happens if Anthropic / OpenAI has an outage?
A: LCEL fallback config: primary on Claude, secondary on GPT, tertiary on a local model for the most critical paths. Automatic 30-second failover; eval set runs against fallbacks weekly so we know they meet our quality bar.

Resume bullets for Project 1

Designed and delivered an Enterprise Knowledge Intelligence Agent on Neo4j + Pinecone — ingesting 50k+ docs across 4 source systems into a constrained-ontology KG with full provenance, and serving cross-document questions via three-lane hybrid retrieval (vector + BM25 + graph) with rerank.
Reduced hallucination ~70% on labeled eval set by enforcing evidence-first answer schema, post-generation groundedness LLM-as-judge, and continuous weekly eval of retrieval recall, citation coverage, and faithfulness.
